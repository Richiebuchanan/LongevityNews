What this is

Longevity News is an automated reading desk for the science of living longer and healthier. Every morning it sweeps aging-research journals, preprint servers, ClinicalTrials.gov, longevity news outlets, and a set of podcasts and YouTube channels; reads what's new; and files each item with two independent grades — how trustworthy the evidence is, and how much it should change what a person actually does. Once a day it also rewrites a six-week synthesis of where the field is moving, with every claim footnoted back to its source.

The baseline

State of the Science is the living document the rest of the site measures against: what fifty years of research established, where the field reversed itself, what sits at the frontier, and a four-tier instruction manual for what to actually do. It is versioned and append-only. The daily run compares new tier-1 and tier-2 evidence against the current version and publishes a new one only when a claim, confidence level or instruction should change — with a changelog entry citing the evidence.

The people

Envelope Pushers profiles the futurists, clinicians, self-experimenters, podcasters, scientists and funders driving the conversation, with each ones current program, claims, personal protocol, an evidence check, and disclosed conflicts of interest.

The two-axis idea

Most longevity coverage collapses "is there evidence?" and "is it safe?" into one impression. This site keeps them apart on purpose, because the answers are independent. Something can have decades of safety data and no evidence it works (most of the supplement aisle). Something else can have the strongest lifespan data in mammals and real side effects (rapamycin). The Interventions page grades each on both axes and combines them into one of four verdicts:

  • Supported — reasonable human evidence it works, and it's safe at studied doses.
  • Works But Weigh The Risk — real evidence of benefit, real side effects or unknowns. Physician territory.
  • Low Downside, Unproven — no good evidence it helps, and no evidence it hurts. If you want to try it, the honest answer is "knock yourself out."
  • Avoid — unproven or disproven, with a documented or serious safety concern.

Evidence tiers on research items

Every paper, preprint and article on Research Watch carries a tier: 1 human RCT or meta-analysis of RCTs; 2 human observational or a single small RCT; 3 animal model; 4 in-vitro or mechanistic; 5 opinion, anecdote or unverified claim. Podcasts and videos are never treated as evidence — they're leads, and each one carries a note flagging where the host or guest ran ahead of the data.

How it's built

A daily scheduled task in Claude does the sweep, classification, scoring and synthesis, writing to a Supabase database. These pages are static HTML on GitHub Pages that read the database directly through a public read-only key and refresh about once a minute. Sources, researchers and interventions are added through a password-gated admin page. The site is a sister project to Surety Market Intelligence, which uses the same architecture for insurance-industry news.

What it is not

Medical advice. The grades are generated by an AI reading published sources, they can be wrong, and none of it accounts for your health history. Talk to your physician before changing anything.
